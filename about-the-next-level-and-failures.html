About the next level and failures

I’ve heard about “getting out of your comfort zone” for decades, but it never really made sense to me. Yes, growing happens when you stop doing the comfortable routine and start doing something else, something challenging you are not used to, but why would you do that in the first place?! It is called a comfort zone for a reason. I mean I get it, but at the same time, it doesn’t make a lot of sense.

Then we have “failures” (or hard-learned lessons), which occur when you do something and that something doesn’t work as expected. I also know not to be harsh with myself when that happens, and I have also heard for years that creating an environment where failing is OK is needed, but that doesn’t make much sense either, because oftentimes these failures are evaluated, and we certainly don’t want to have a negative evaluation.

Then I got it! Putting the two together, it is this growing thing, this performing to the next level. You don’t quite fail when you’re trying something you’re very accustomed to doing, and you will certainly “fail” when you’re doing something that is harder to do. It sounds so obvious, but this harder thing is getting out of the comfort zone, not just for the sake of it, but because we have already mastered the (now) easy thing, and we are trying something harder.

Think of running. If you have never run, walking is easy; it is the comfort zone. Going for a 1-mile short run is really hard! Then, when that gets easy, you go to a 5k, 10k, half marathon, or a marathon! Every step of the way gets harder than the previous one, but at the same time and with time and practice, it gets easier and easier. Because you have already prepared for the next level, you are performing at the next level.

So, failure is not only an option but a requirement; it is unavoidable, and you should expect it and want it. Over time that will become your comfort zone, and you’ll be ready for the next one.
