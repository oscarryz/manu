Things to do while we wait for the end of the world

Or at least the end of our world. Or maybe the end of our lives? The end of my life? I’m not sure. What is certain is I’m not going to live forever. That is as far as the perception of the world goes for better or for worse. I am not aware of what happened before I was born (or the first few years after that) and I definitely won’t know what is going to happen after I die, so from a perception point of view, that is the world.

Anyhow. So, what to do while that moment arrives? (Which, needless to say, it is impossible for me to know.) For one, keep breathing. Look around me (around you; I am at this point not clear if I should write in the first or third person, etc.) and let the senses enjoy what they sense. As the movie “At the end of the senses” (googles the title… ha, interesting, I thought the movie title was “At the end of the senses” — which sounds odd, btw, but the actual title was “Perfect Sense”. I got that title from the title it received in Mexico: “Al final de los sentidos”. Interesting. Going back to my point) — ehem, “Perfect Sense” — things happen or not through our senses, and we should enjoy them (or use them) while we still have them, which is almost the same amount of time as our lives; smell the flowers (or the dust), feel the warmth of my clothes on a cold day (or the cold on my cheeks because I can’t protect my whole face), see the beautiful blue sky and the contrast with the city bricks, hear the people around me, and sip this hot coffee while enjoying right now.

Of course, we are not just sensorial beings, we are not only meant to sense things, but also to “feel” them (interpret them), share them with our loved ones — and acquaintances all the same —, use our imagination, talk about them, create new ideas, change our minds, get excited, feel sadness. There are so many things we can do, even if we can’t do them all.

So, yes, everything has an end, but that doesn’t mean we cannot enjoy things while they last, prepare or look for better things, or endure the hard times only for the sake of overcoming them. After all, the end, wherever it might happen, will always be there, waiting for us. Not like a predestined fate, but as an unavoidable and moving target. It is not prewritten; we write it as we go, and we can go as far as we try, even if the attempt is just to sit and wait. That is also a choice.
